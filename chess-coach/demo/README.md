# Demo corpora

Two of them, and the difference matters.

- **`games.pgn` here** is synthetic engine self-play. It exercises the pipeline
  and doubles as a negative control.
- **[`real/`](real/)** is fourteen actual blitz games plus a context file, and
  is the worked example for `profile --context`.

## This corpus — synthetic, not a real player

`games.pgn` is **engine self-play**, not anybody's games: two deliberately
weakened Stockfish instances (Skill Level 3 vs 4) playing out of real opening
lines, generated by `tools/simulate.py`. It exists so the pipeline can be
exercised end to end without a network connection or somebody's private games.

The reports here are therefore examples of **format and behaviour**, not
findings about a human being. Two specific things to keep in mind:

- The clock comments are synthetic and uncorrelated with move quality. That is
  actually useful as a negative control: the time-based detectors correctly
  stay silent on this corpus, which is what should happen when the timing data
  carries no signal.
- Three games hit the simulator's 80-move cap, so they have no recorded result
  and show up as "no recorded result" in the report.

Regenerate everything with:

```bash
.venv/bin/python tools/simulate.py --games 14 --out demo/games.pgn
.venv/bin/chess-coach analyse demo/games.pgn --user retsekoj_demo -o demo/analysis.json
.venv/bin/chess-coach profile demo/analysis.json -o demo/profile.json
.venv/bin/chess-coach report  demo/profile.json --html demo/report.html --md demo/report.md
.venv/bin/chess-coach dossier demo/profile.json -o demo/dossier.md
```

Analysing these 14 games took about 13 minutes on four cores.
